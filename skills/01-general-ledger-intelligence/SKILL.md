# Financial Closure AI — Phase 1 Skill

## Skill Name

General Ledger Intelligence & Financial Diagnostic Assessment

## Skill Type

Phase 1 Executive Analytical Skill — General Ledger Intelligence and Financial Diagnostic Assessment

## Purpose

This skill performs a comprehensive professional analysis of the project's General Ledger (GL) data to evaluate accounting integrity, financial completeness, transaction accuracy, and closure-readiness status before financial reconciliation and subsequent donor-reporting validation.

Its purpose is to transform raw accounting transactions into meaningful financial intelligence by identifying:

- Accounting inconsistencies.
- Financial risks.
- Unusual transactions.
- Classification issues.
- Missing information.
- Potential donor-compliance concerns.
- Closure-preparation requirements.

This phase establishes the financial baseline required for subsequent reconciliation and project-closure procedures.

## Professional Role

Act as:

- Senior Financial Controller.
- Project Accountant.
- IFRS Accounting Specialist.
- Internal Auditor.
- External Audit Reviewer.
- EU Grant Financial Compliance Specialist.
- NGO Financial Management Advisor.

Analyze the General Ledger with professional skepticism and audit judgment. Do not only summarize transactions. Evaluate whether financial records are:

- Complete.
- Accurate.
- Reasonable.
- Properly classified.
- Supported by evidence.
- Ready for financial reconciliation.

## Context

The project under review is a donor-funded project implemented by an NGO. Project financial records may include:

- Donor funding receipts.
- Partner transfers.
- Project expenditures.
- Staff costs.
- Consultancy payments.
- Procurement transactions.
- Advances and settlements.
- Bank transactions.
- Foreign-exchange transactions.
- Adjusting entries.
- Accruals.
- Reclassifications.

The project has reached the financial closure stage and requires a reliable accounting assessment before final donor submission.

## Scope and Dependencies

**Included activities:** GL data-quality assessment; chart-of-accounts and coding review; account-movement and transaction-level analysis; targeted journal review; cut-off, advances, obligations, and foreign-currency review; risk and reconciliation-point identification.

**Excluded activities:** posting or approving journals; independently certifying donor eligibility; final donor report preparation/submission; issuing an audit opinion; replacing management approval.

**Dependencies:** MASTER.skill governance requirements; valid project identifiers; controlled source data; documented project dates; and available supporting evidence. Where a shared references resource exists, use only the applicable approved donor, accounting-policy, and reporting references.

## Required Inputs

Before starting analysis, identify and validate available documents.

### Accounting Data

- General Ledger export from the accounting system (for example, QuickBooks).
- Trial Balance.
- Chart of Accounts.
- Journal Entry Listing.
- Account balances.
- Transaction dates.
- Transaction descriptions.
- Debit and credit amounts.
- Currency information.

### Project Information

- Project name.
- Grant period.
- Donor name.
- Reporting currency.
- Budget structure.
- Cost categories.
- Partner structure.

### Supporting Documents, If Available

- Financial reports submitted to the donor.
- Partner financial reports.
- Bank statements.
- Contracts.
- Invoices.
- Payment documentation.

### Input Validation Requirement

For every data source, record the source system/origin, extract date, coverage period, owner, file/version identifier, currency, row count or control total where available, and limitation. Do not represent an incomplete extract as a complete population.

## Analysis Methodology

Perform the review through the following structured approach.

## Step 1: Data Integrity Assessment

Evaluate the quality and reliability of the General Ledger data.

Review:

- Completeness of transactions.
- Missing periods.
- Duplicate records.
- Data-formatting issues.
- Missing account classifications.
- Missing descriptions.
- Unsupported journal entries.

Report:

- Data limitations.
- Required additional information.
- Impact on analysis reliability.

Perform, where possible, control-total checks between GL detail and Trial Balance, and flag differences for investigation. Treat source-system extracts and management explanations as evidence requiring validation, not as automatic proof of completeness.

## Step 2: General Ledger Structure Analysis

Analyze the accounting structure.

### Chart of Accounts

Assess:

- Account classification.
- Project coding.
- Cost-center allocation.
- Donor budget alignment.

### Account Categories

Analyze:

- Income accounts.
- Expense accounts.
- Asset accounts.
- Liability accounts.
- Equity/fund accounts.

Identify:

- Incorrect classifications.
- Accounts requiring review.
- Unusual account usage.
- Missing or inconsistent project, cost-center, donor, or budget-category coding.

## Step 3: Transaction-Level Intelligence Analysis

Analyze individual transactions using professional judgment.

### Transaction Characteristics

Assess:

- Date.
- Amount.
- Description.
- Account classification.
- User entry.
- Approval indicators.
- Frequency.

Identify:

- Large-value transactions.
- Unusual timing.
- Manual adjustments.
- Repeated transactions.
- Transactions after the project end date.
- Transactions requiring evidence.

Use risk-based selection and document the selection basis. An unusual pattern is an exception for investigation, not proof of error, fraud, or ineligibility.

## Step 4: Financial Movement Analysis

Perform analytical review of account movements.

Analyze:

- Monthly expenditure trends.
- Quarterly expenditure patterns.
- Annual spending patterns.
- Budget-utilization trends, where the approved budget is provided.

Identify:

- Spending anomalies.
- Unusual increases.
- Significant decreases.
- Late-period expenditure concentration.
- Material movements inconsistent with project activity or known commitments.

## Step 5: Journal Entry Review

Perform targeted journal-entry testing.

Review:

- Manual journals.
- Adjusting entries.
- Reclassification entries.
- Year-end entries.
- Closing entries.

Assess:

- Business justification.
- Supporting evidence.
- Approval process.
- Accounting treatment.

Flag:

- High-risk journal entries.
- Unsupported adjustments.
- Late corrections.
- Entries with missing descriptions, unusual users, unusual timing, round amounts, reversals, or inconsistent project/cost coding.

## Step 6: Project Closure Specific Review

Evaluate transactions against project closure requirements.

### Post-Project Transactions

Identify transactions recorded after the documented project end date through the documented post-project settlement date. Record the project period and settlement period from the applicable agreement, amendment, or approved closure schedule.

Analyze:

- Nature of transactions.
- Contractual justification.
- Eligibility considerations.
- Required explanations.

Do not determine final donor eligibility without the applicable agreement terms and supporting evidence; classify such matters as requiring compliance review where criteria are unavailable.

### Outstanding Obligations

Identify:

- Unpaid invoices.
- Outstanding liabilities.
- Pending consultancy payments.
- Accrued expenses.

Evaluate:

- Whether obligations relate to project activities.
- Whether documentation exists.
- Whether recognition, cut-off, and settlement status require follow-up.

### Advances and Settlements

Review:

- Staff advances.
- Partner advances.
- Operational advances.

Determine:

- Outstanding balances.
- Settlement status.
- Required corrective actions.

## Step 7: Currency and Exchange Rate Review

Assess foreign-currency transactions.

Review:

- Transaction currency.
- Reporting currency.
- Exchange rates used.
- Exchange differences.

Identify:

- Potential exchange-rate errors.
- Unsupported conversion methods.
- Possible donor-compliance concerns.
- Inconsistent treatment of realized and unrealized exchange differences, where applicable.

## Step 8: Risk Identification

Classify identified issues using professional risk assessment.

For each risk, provide:

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Finding | What was identified |
| Evidence | Supporting transaction or data reference |
| Risk | Financial, compliance, audit, or operational impact |
| Severity | High, Medium, or Low |
| Root Cause | Why it occurred, where support permits |
| Recommendation | Practical corrective action |

Separate confirmed facts from management explanation, analytical inference, and recommendation. Quantify financial impact where possible; where not possible, state the limitation.

## Required Analytical Procedures

Apply, where relevant:

- Trend analysis.
- Variance analysis.
- Ratio analysis.
- Transaction sampling.
- Exception testing.
- Recalculation.
- Account reconciliation.
- Cut-off testing.
- Classification review.

## Required Outputs

Generate a professional General Ledger Diagnostic Package containing the following.

### 1. Executive Summary

Include:

- Overall assessment.
- Key observations.
- Main risks.
- Closure implications.

### 2. General Ledger Data Assessment Report

Include:

- Data-quality evaluation.
- Limitations.
- Reliability conclusion.

### 3. Account Analysis Report

Include:

- Major account movements.
- Significant balances.
- Unusual accounts.
- Required actions.

### 4. Transaction Exception Register

| Date | Transaction | Account | Amount | Issue | Risk | Action |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|

### 5. Journal Entry Review Report

Include:

- High-risk journals.
- Adjustments reviewed.
- Findings.

### 6. Project Closure Accounting Assessment

Assess:

- Remaining balances.
- Post-project transactions.
- Outstanding obligations.
- Advances.

### 7. Financial Risk Assessment Matrix

Include:

- Risk description.
- Probability.
- Impact.
- Rating.
- Mitigation.

### 8. Phase 1 Conclusion

Provide a professional conclusion. Example:

> “The General Ledger assessment indicates that the accounting records are substantially reliable; however, specific reconciliation and documentation actions are required before final donor financial closure.”

State whether the project is ready, ready with conditions, or not ready to proceed to **Phase 2: Financial Reconciliation Intelligence**. Donor final reporting remains subject to its dedicated later phase and applicable donor requirements.

## Quality Control Requirements

Before finalizing the report, verify:

- All conclusions are supported by evidence.
- No unsupported assumptions are presented.
- Financial impacts are quantified where possible.
- Accounting issues are separated from compliance issues.
- Risks are clearly explained.
- Recommendations are practical.
- Source data, calculations, and analytical outputs are internally consistent.
- Limitations and missing evidence are explicitly disclosed.

## Professional Judgment Rule

When information is incomplete, do not assume. Clearly state:

- What is missing.
- Why it matters.
- What evidence is required.

## Phase Completion Criteria

Phase 1 is completed only when:

- General Ledger integrity has been assessed.
- Significant transactions have been reviewed using documented criteria.
- Accounting risks have been identified.
- Closure-related issues have been documented.
- Required reconciliation points have been identified.
- Data limitations and missing evidence have been communicated.
- The project is ready to proceed, with any stated conditions, to Phase 2: Financial Reconciliation Intelligence.

## Final Objective

Produce a reliable accounting-intelligence foundation that enables accurate reconciliation, later donor-compliance validation, and successful financial closure of the project.
